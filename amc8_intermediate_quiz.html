QUESTION 1Word Problem

A bookstore sells notebooks for $4 each and pens for $2 each. Maya buys 11 items and spends $32. How many notebooks does she buy?

Let n be the number of notebooks. The number of pens is 11 − n, and the total cost is $32.
QUESTION 2Geometry

A rectangle has perimeter 34 cm. Its length is 3 cm greater than its width. What is its area?

Half the perimeter is the sum of the length and width. Use the 3 cm difference to find both dimensions.
QUESTION 3Probability

A bag contains 5 red, 3 blue, and 2 green marbles. One marble is drawn at random. What is the probability that it is NOT blue?

Count all the marbles that satisfy the condition, then divide by the total number of marbles.
QUESTION 4Combinatorics

A club has 6 students. In how many different ways can it choose a president and a vice president if no student can hold both positions?

Choose the president first. Then count how many students remain eligible to be vice president.
QUESTION 5Number Theory

What is the least positive integer that is divisible by 6, 8, and 15?

Find the least common multiple by comparing the prime factorizations of the three numbers.
QUESTION 6Word Problem

A cyclist rides at 12 miles per hour for 30 minutes and then at 8 miles per hour for 45 minutes. What is the cyclist's average speed for the entire trip?

Average speed is total distance divided by total time, not necessarily the average of the two speeds.
QUESTION 7Geometry

A square has side length 10 cm. A smaller square has side length 6 cm. What is the area of the region inside the larger square but outside the smaller square?

Find each square's area separately, then subtract the smaller area from the larger area.
QUESTION 8Counting & Probability

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that their sum is 9?

List the ordered pairs that add to 9. Rolling a 3 then a 6 differs from rolling a 6 then a 3.
QUESTION 9Number Theory

A positive integer n leaves a remainder of 2 when divided by 5 and a remainder of 1 when divided by 3. What is the smallest possible value of n?

List numbers of the form 5k + 2 in increasing order, then test which one leaves remainder 1 when divided by 3.
QUESTION 10Combinatorics

How many different three-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, and 4 if no digit may be repeated?

Count the choices for each position in order. Each time you use a digit, one fewer digit remains available.
